# The-Political-Role-of-Fake-News-and-Fake-News-Efficacy
This research project investigates the concept of citizens' confidence in their ability to identify political bias and misinformation - fake news efficacy (FNE) - and its relationship with basic demographics, institutional trust, and electoral engagement. Using an original survey (n = 2000) fielded during the 2022 Hungarian national election, we find that FNE is strongly asociated with higher confidence in partisan-aligned political institutions and higher levels of electoral engagement. Among supports of the autocrizer's opposition, FNE is associated with higher confidence in their party and increase intent to vote in the upcoming election. 

This article was a collaboration between Professors Laura Jakli, Matthew Stenberg, Sivaram Cheruvu, and Jay N. Krehbiel where I served as lead Research Associate. My primary role was to develop the R code for generating the multitude of tables and figures that translated the results from the survey data into publication-ready visualiziations.
